Cabinet painting vs. replacement: when refinishing makes sense

Practical guidance from R Painters LLC for Charleston and Lowcountry property owners.

Replacing kitchen cabinets can completely change a room, but replacement is not the only way to update a kitchen. When the existing cabinet boxes and doors are in good structural condition, professional cabinet painting or refinishing can provide a major visual change without rebuilding the entire layout.

Cabinet painting is usually about the finish—not structural changes

Refinishing works best when you like the existing layout and the cabinets are solid enough to keep. It changes the color and finish while preserving the cabinet boxes, doors and overall configuration.

Preparation is the difference between wall paint and cabinet work

Cabinets are high-touch surfaces exposed to grease, moisture and regular cleaning. A cabinet project typically requires degreasing, sanding or deglossing, repairs where appropriate, bonding primer when needed and a durable cabinet-grade finish. The process is more detailed than simply rolling wall paint onto a door.

Sprayed finish or roller-applied finish?

Both application methods can be appropriate depending on the project, budget and desired finish. Spraying can produce a very smooth appearance when doors and drawer fronts are properly prepared and controlled. A carefully applied roller-and-brush system can also provide a durable professional result while reducing the setup required for spraying in some occupied homes.

When replacement may be the better choice

  • Cabinet boxes are damaged or failing
  • You want to completely change the kitchen layout
  • Doors or drawer boxes need extensive structural work
  • You want features that the existing cabinets cannot reasonably accommodate

What to evaluate before requesting an estimate

Count the approximate number of doors and drawers, note whether the current finish is painted, stained or factory-coated, and identify damaged areas. Photos are especially useful for cabinet estimates because they help us understand the door style and scope before the walkthrough.
